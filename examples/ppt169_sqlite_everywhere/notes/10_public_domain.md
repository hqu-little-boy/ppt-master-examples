The second trust factor is legal, and it is the simplest one in this deck: there is no license to comply with. In the project's own words, anyone is free to copy, modify, publish, use, compile, sell, or distribute the original SQLite code for any purpose, commercial or non-commercial, and by any means. That holds because every author signs an affidavit dedicating their work to the public domain, with the originals kept in a firesafe at Hwaci, and because every line is written from scratch and traceable to its author. The consequence is that SQLite is open-source but not open-contribution: no patch is accepted without such an affidavit. And for organisations in places that do not recognise the public domain, Hwaci sells a Warranty of Title, and the proceeds fund SQLite's continued development.
